CAUTI / CLABSI — AMR Burden

Healthcare-associated infection pathogens and their resistance profiles (2022–2024)

CAUTI (Catheter-associated UTI):
E. coli and K. pneumoniae predominate. In ICU CAUTIs, carbapenem resistance: K. pneumoniae ~75%, E. coli ~45%.


CLABSI (Central-line BSI):
Staphylococci (27%), Enterobacterales (38%), NFGNB (22%). MRSA in CLABSI: 87% of S. aureus isolates in ICUs (2022).


VAP (Ventilator-associated pneumonia):
A. baumannii and P. aeruginosa dominant (>60% of LRT isolates in ICUs). Carbapenem resistance in ICU LRT: A. baumannii >90%, P. aeruginosa ~45%.


SSI (Surgical site infection):
Gram-negatives account for 76% of SSIs — higher than historical assumptions. E. coli (ESBL-producing) is leading SSI pathogen.

S. Typhi — Fluoroquinolone Resistance Mutation Cascade

Progressive gyrA/parC mutations correlating with ciprofloxacin MIC escalation (2017–2024)

Mutation cascade driving CIP resistance in S. Typhi:
Step 1: Single gyrA S83F → MIC 0.12–1 µg/ml (low-level, still treatable)
Step 2: gyrA S83F + D87N → MIC 1–4 µg/ml (intermediate resistance)
Step 3: + parC S80I → MIC 6–32 µg/ml (clinical failure)
Step 4: + PMQR genes → MIC 32–256 µg/ml (high-level, untreatable by FQ)
By 2024: MIC90 = 128 µg/ml. >90% of FQ-R isolates carry gyrA S83F as primary mutation.

MRSA Resistance Mechanisms & Treatment Options

mecA/mecC gene mediated oxacillin resistance · Linezolid resistance emerging in E. faecium · Vancomycin remains fully active in S. aureus

mecA Gene
Universal in MRSA isolates
Encodes PBP2a (penicillin-binding protein 2a) with low affinity for all β-lactams. SCCmec types II, III, IV predominant in India.
Vancomycin — Fully Preserved
VRSA: 0% — never detected
No vancomycin-resistant S. aureus (VRSA) reported in the entire 8-year ICMR AMRSN network. Vancomycin MIC creep monitored but not clinically significant.
Linezolid Resistance — Emerging
~3.4% linezolid R in MRSA (2024)
23S rRNA mutations and cfr gene. Rising from near-zero baseline. In E. faecium: 9.1% linezolid resistant (2024). Threatens the last reliable oral option for MRSA.
colistin mcr Gene
mcr-1 prevalence: <2%
Plasmid-mediated colistin resistance (mcr-1, mcr-3) detected but at very low prevalence. Intrinsic chromosomal mechanisms also contribute. K. pneumoniae colistin R fell from 13% (2017) to 4% (2024).

Why ICU Resistance Is So Much Higher — Key Drivers

🔬 Prior Antibiotic Exposure

ICU patients typically arrive after failed community treatment — selecting for resistant organisms already. Multiple prior courses of carbapenems, cephalosporins create selective pressure.
🏥 Cross-Transmission

ICU environments concentrate resistant organisms. Medical devices (ventilators, central lines, urinary catheters) act as reservoirs. A. baumannii persists on surfaces for weeks.
🛡️ Compromised Immunity

ICU patients have impaired immune defences (surgery, trauma, organ failure, immunosuppression) — allowing colonisation by resistant organisms that OPD patients would clear.
📊 Selection Bias

ICU specimens are more likely to be from patients already failing therapy — so resistant organisms are disproportionately cultured. OPD isolates include many first-presentation patients.

Key Fungal AMR Findings from ICMR AMRSN Reports

🍄 C. tropicalis Dominance
India differs from global pattern
C. tropicalis is the most common yeast in India (vs C. albicans globally). It carries higher virulence and higher azole resistance. Increasing caspofungin resistance further limits options.
🚨 C. auris Emergence
Multi-drug resistant nosocomial pathogen
C. auris causes nosocomial outbreaks, environmentally persistent, >90% fluconazole resistant. Some isolates resistant to all 3 antifungal classes. ICMR network tracking since ~2019.
📈 Echinocandin Pressure
Caspofungin resistance rising in C. tropicalis
ICMR 2017 report flagged increasing caspofungin resistance in C. tropicalis as a "serious concern." FKS gene mutations drive resistance. Limits salvage therapy for azole-resistant candidemia.
🦠 Wickerhamomyces Emerging
Recognized in pediatric patients (2017)
Wickerhamomyces anomalous (13.1% of yeast in pediatric specimens, 2017) is an emerging uncommon pathogen with variable antifungal susceptibility.
🌿 Aspergillus flavus
Most common mold — India-specific pattern
A. flavus is most common mold in India (vs A. fumigatus globally). Mucormycosis surged post-COVID (2021–22 CAM wave). Voriconazole azole resistance in Aspergillus remains low but monitored.
💊 Treatment Implications
Empiric fluconazole unreliable for candidemia
Given >60% fluconazole resistance in C. tropicalis (dominant species), empiric echinocandins are preferred for candidemia in India. C. albicans remains relatively susceptible to fluconazole.

About This Dashboard

This dashboard visualizes 8 years of antimicrobial resistance surveillance data from the ICMR Antimicrobial Resistance Research and Surveillance Network (AMRSN). Data were extracted from the annual reports published for years 2017 through 2024.

Data Sources

  • ICMR AMRSN Annual Reports 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 (8th Edition)
  • Primary source: Chapter 1 summary tables and yearly trend tables in each report
  • Multi-year trend tables cross-verified from 2023 and 2024 reports (which contain cumulative trend data)

Antibiotic Classes Tracked

  • Carbapenems — Imipenem, Meropenem, Ertapenem
  • Fluoroquinolones — Ciprofloxacin
  • 3rd-gen Cephalosporins — Cefotaxime, Ceftriaxone, Ceftazidime
  • Aminoglycosides — Amikacin, Gentamicin
  • β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors — Piperacillin-Tazobactam
  • Last-resort agents — Colistin/Polymyxin B, Vancomycin, Linezolid, Minocycline

Important Caveats

  • All data are from ICMR tertiary-care hospitals; not representative of community resistance.
  • The surveillance network grew from 8 centres (2017) to 30+ sites (2024), affecting comparability.
  • 2018–2019 data for some pathogen–antibiotic pairs were estimated by interpolation between 2017 and 2020 values where not directly reported in summary tables.
  • 2020 data affected by COVID-19 pandemic (reduced admissions, altered case mix).
  • Carbapenem resistance definition varies slightly (imipenem vs meropenem); imipenem used as default where available.
  • The original ICMR AMRSN annual reports are authoritative; this is a secondary compilation for exploration.
